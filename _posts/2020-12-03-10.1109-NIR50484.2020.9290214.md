## Overview

The spring-loaded inverted pendulum (SLIP) models running as a body mass supported by a leg that compresses on contact and rebounds before liftoff. Zashchitin and colleagues use a single-leg hopper to compare two ways of reproducing that behavior: program it into the controller, or build it into the linkage.

## Mechanism Analysis

The **open-chain hopper** uses a serial leg and impedance control. The controller shapes how the leg responds to contact, making it yield and rebound like a spring rather than simply follow a rigid position command.

The **closed-chain hopper** uses an underactuated linkage designed around the SLIP model. The linkage supplies part of the desired response mechanically, while the actuators use position and velocity control. The comparison therefore changes the mechanism and controller together; it is not a test of linkage topology with all other design choices held constant.

## Results

The paper evaluates the two hoppers in simulation. The [IEEE abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9290214) describes both approaches as producing passive, spring-like ground interaction, but does not give numerical energy results. This comparison should not be read as experimental evidence for lower electrical energy consumption on a physical robot or as a ranking of open- and closed-chain mechanisms in general.

## Conclusion

The study illustrates how a linkage can supply part of the contact behavior normally assigned to an impedance controller. For a new robot, the useful design question is which combination of mechanism and control delivers the required motion and energy use, rather than which topology is more efficient in isolation.
