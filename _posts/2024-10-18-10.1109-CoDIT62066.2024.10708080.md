## Overview

Model predictive control (MPC) chooses a mobile robot's next action by predicting how the robot will respond to candidate commands. The score used to rank those commands matters: two controllers can predict future motion in a similar way but evaluate it differently. This study compares conventional MPC with a Q-learning predictive controller for mobile robot navigation.

## Methodology

Conventional MPC uses the robot model to roll out candidate action sequences and evaluates the running objective along those predictions. The learning controller keeps predictive action selection but uses predicted action-value estimates, or Q-values, in place of the running-objective rollouts. A Q-value estimates the future return from taking an action in a given state. The study changes how the controller scores predicted actions rather than replacing prediction with a model-free policy.

The [authors' simulation repository](https://github.com/thd-research/RL-autonomous-navigation) documents a ROS and Gazebo setup with selectable controller modes and parameters for the planning horizon and sampling period.

## Results

The authors report in the [IEEE abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10708080) that the Q-learning predictive controller outperformed the conventional MPC baseline in their navigation experiments. They also report the same computational complexity for the action-sequence calculation in the two controllers. That comparison concerns one step in each algorithm, not measured runtime or the cost of learning the Q-function.

The public abstract does not give the performance metric or the size of the gain. This is a comparison with conventional MPC, not evidence of faster learning than model-free methods or of robustness to model error.

## Conclusion

The comparison shows that the choice of scoring function can matter even when a controller still plans over predicted actions. The reported gain supports testing learned action values within MPC on similar tasks; it does not establish a general performance or safety guarantee.
