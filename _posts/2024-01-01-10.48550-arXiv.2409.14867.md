## Overview

Reinforcement learning agents frequently explore unsafe or irrelevant regions of the state space during training.
In robotics, such exploration is not only inefficient — it can be hazardous.
A common mitigation is shielding: constraining RL actions to a pre-defined safe set.
The difficulty is that overly tight constraints suppress exploration and slow learning, while loose constraints fail to provide safety guarantees.
This paper introduces **CALF** (Critic As Lyapunov Function), a model-free RL agent that maintains formal goal-reaching guarantees throughout learning without restricting which state-action pairs are explorable.

## Theoretical Basis

CALF re-interprets the critic (action-value function) as a surrogate Lyapunov function and imposes a Lyapunov descent condition as a soft constraint on critic updates.
Under standard assumptions, this constraint ensures that the policy sequence produced during learning preserves the goal-reaching property of the baseline policy $\pi_0$.
The formal result (Theorem 1 in the paper) states that the agent converges to the target set with high probability regardless of the specific RL optimiser or critic architecture employed.

The guarantee holds in stochastic environments and requires no explicit model of system dynamics — the structure is entirely embedded in the critic training procedure.
All state-action pairs remain accessible for exploration, so the guarantee is achieved without the exploration restriction that limits conventional shielded RL methods.

## Experimental Validation

CALF was evaluated on a real TurtleBot3 Burger performing point navigation in a planar environment with a designated hazardous area in which the robot's speed was penalised.
The learning objective was to minimise cumulative cost while guaranteeing convergence to the goal.

Baselines: Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO), a modified SARSA without fallback recovery (SARSA-m), a nominal polar-coordinate controller, and a model predictive controller (MPC) with full dynamics access.
All agents were initialised identically and trained over a deliberately limited number of episodes to expose differences in sample efficiency.

CALF achieved a 100% goal-reaching success rate across episodes, outperforming PPO and SARSA-m in both convergence reliability and accumulated cost. Its performance approached that of MPC, despite operating without a model and at substantially lower computational cost.

## Conclusion

CALF demonstrates that Lyapunov-based guarantees — traditionally associated with model-based or restricted control classes — are achievable in a fully model-free, data-driven RL framework.
By embedding the Lyapunov descent condition into critic training rather than into action filtering, the method retains full explorability while providing formal goal-reaching assurance.
This opens a principled path toward safe RL for physical robotic systems where both exploration and safety guarantees are required simultaneously.
