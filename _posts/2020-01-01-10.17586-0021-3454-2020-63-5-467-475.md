## Overview

Adaptive grippers must conform to objects of varying geometry without requiring a separate controller for each shape class.
The underlying mechanism design problem — choosing both the topology of the kinematic chain and the lengths of its links — is typically treated in two disconnected steps: structural synthesis selects the linkage topology, and parametric optimisation then tunes the geometric parameters.
This paper proposes a unified algorithm that performs structural and parametric synthesis jointly, with the additional capability of variable-length links that can reconfigure the gripper's reach and compliance during operation.

## Design Method

The algorithm operates in two coupled phases. In the structural phase, a set of admissible planar linkage topologies is generated based on mobility criteria and assembly constraints. In the parametric phase, link lengths are treated not as fixed design constants but as continuous variables that may also be adjusted at runtime through actuated prismatic joints embedded in selected links.

This variable-length formulation expands the gripper's reachable configuration space significantly: a single mechanism can produce prehensile configurations adapted to objects of substantially different sizes and shapes without requiring a complete mechanical redesign. The synthesis procedure minimises a cost function combining contact force isotropy, workspace coverage, and mechanical advantage across a representative set of target object geometries.

The paper demonstrates the algorithm on a finger mechanism with one variable-length link and derives the mapping from object geometry to the required link extension profile.

## Contributions

The central contribution is a tractable algorithm that searches the combined structural and parametric design space while respecting kinematic feasibility and actuation constraints. By admitting variable-length links, the design space includes mechanisms that are unreachable by classical fixed-geometry synthesis, and the resulting designs exhibit adaptive grasping behaviour with a minimal actuator count. Formal conditions are derived under which the variable-length extension is uniquely solvable for a given target contact configuration.

## Conclusion

Treating link length as an additional degree of freedom in mechanism synthesis opens a new dimension of adaptability for gripper design. The proposed algorithm provides a systematic route from functional requirements to a concrete mechanism design, bridging the gap between topology selection and parameter tuning. The approach is extensible to spatial linkages and multi-finger architectures.
