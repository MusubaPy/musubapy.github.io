## Overview

Model-free reinforcement learning for robot navigation is well-studied but inherently sample-hungry: without access to a system model, the agent must explore extensively before converging to a useful policy.
Model-based RL (MBRL) addresses this by incorporating learned or known dynamics into the policy update, reducing the number of real-world interactions required.
This paper presents an experimental study of MBRL methods applied to mobile robot navigation, providing empirical evidence for the practical performance gains achievable with model-based approaches.

## Approach

The study evaluates MBRL agents in a navigation task on a non-holonomic mobile robot platform.
The agents use model information — either a known kinematic model or a learned approximation — within their policy optimisation loop.
A standardised evaluation protocol is applied across all tested methods, controlling for initial conditions, episode length, and the number of real-robot interactions, so that performance differences reflect the algorithms rather than experimental variance.

The comparison includes model-free baselines alongside MBRL variants, allowing a direct quantification of the benefit obtained from model access under physical deployment conditions where episodes are limited.

## Results

The experimental results confirm that model-based approaches achieve lower accumulated cost and faster convergence relative to model-free baselines under the same interaction budget.
The advantage is most pronounced in the early training phase, where model-free methods are still exploring and MBRL agents already produce near-feasible trajectories by leveraging model predictions.

The study also examines the sensitivity of MBRL performance to model accuracy, quantifying the degradation that occurs when the model used by the agent deviates from the true robot dynamics. This characterises the operating conditions under which MBRL remains robust.

## Conclusion

The experimental evidence confirms that MBRL is not only theoretically appealing but practically effective for mobile robot navigation with limited training data.
The results provide practitioners with concrete performance benchmarks and a characterisation of the model-accuracy requirements that allow MBRL to outperform model-free approaches in physical robot settings.
