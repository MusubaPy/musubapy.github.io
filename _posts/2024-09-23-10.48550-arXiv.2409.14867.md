## Overview

An online reinforcement learning agent must explore before it can improve a controller. A robot may already have a policy that reaches the goal reliably but follows an inefficient path. Can the agent learn a lower-cost route without sacrificing the baseline's reliability?

This study extends [Critic as Lyapunov Function (CALF)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09869) to stochastic dynamics and evaluates its use on a differential-drive TurtleBot3 Burger.

## Theoretical Basis

CALF accepts a candidate action when it can update the critic subject to Lyapunov-like bounds and a minimum decrease from the last accepted critic value. When it cannot find a feasible update, it retains the previous critic and acts according to the baseline $\pi_0$. The agent does not require a model of the transition dynamics, and the algorithm does not impose a fixed set of prohibited state–action pairs.

Theorem 1 shows that if the baseline approaches the goal set arbitrarily closely with probability at least $1-\eta$ from every initial state, the policy produced by CALF preserves the same lower bound under the paper's assumptions. This is a goal-reaching result, not a guarantee that the robot avoids every hazardous state on the way.

## Experimental Validation

The task is to bring the robot to a target pose near a circular region that both raises cost and caps the robot's speed at 1 cm/s. The authors compare CALF with its nominal polar-coordinate controller, modified SARSA (SARSA-m), proximal policy optimization (PPO), and a model predictive controller (MPC) with a 10-step horizon. PPO and SARSA-m were pretrained to roughly match the nominal controller's initial performance.

Across the simulated runs with 20 random seeds, Figure 4 reports goal-reaching rates of 100% for CALF, no more than 30% for PPO, and about 50% for SARSA-m. CALF also achieved lower accumulated cost than both learning baselines and approached MPC's performance in this task. The hardware runs reproduced selected best episodes; the success rates are from simulation, not repeated hardware trials.

## Conclusion

CALF separates the role of the baseline, which provides the goal-reaching guarantee, from that of the critic, which learns to reduce task cost. The TurtleBot3 case study illustrates the resulting sample-efficiency gain in one navigation setting, without establishing a general advantage over model predictive control.
