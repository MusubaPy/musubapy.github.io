## Overview

Underactuated robotic systems — those with fewer independent actuators than degrees of freedom — require carefully constructed control strategies because direct state tracking is infeasible through actuation alone.
Reinforcement learning offers a route to control such systems without requiring a precise model, but the reward design problem is non-trivial: a naïve reward that penalises final-state error fails to guide the agent through the complex swing-up and stabilisation sequence that underactuated dynamics demand.
The situation is further complicated when system parameters (link masses, lengths, friction) are uncertain, as the agent must produce policies robust to this uncertainty.
This paper proposes **Greedy-Divide and Conquer** — a reward planning algorithm that decomposes the task into sub-goals and constructs a reward signal that guides the agent through each sequentially.

## Approach

The algorithm partitions the overall task objective into a sequence of sub-goals ordered by proximity to the final target state. At each planning step, the greedy strategy selects the next sub-goal as the reachable state closest to the target that can be attained from the current region. The "divide" step identifies this sub-goal structure; the "conquer" step constructs a local reward function for each segment.

Under parameter uncertainty, the reward planning procedure accounts for the range of plausible system behaviours by constructing sub-goal regions (rather than point targets) that remain reachable across the uncertainty set. This yields a reward signal that is robust to model mismatch without requiring explicit worst-case optimisation.

The approach is demonstrated on an underactuated benchmark system where the nonlinear dynamics make direct policy search without reward shaping ineffective.

## Results

The proposed algorithm is evaluated against standard reward formulations (weighted error penalties, sparse goal rewards) on the target underactuated system under varying levels of parameter uncertainty. The Greedy-Divide and Conquer reward consistently achieves higher success rates and faster convergence, with the advantage growing as parameter uncertainty increases.

The results demonstrate that task decomposition embedded in the reward signal is an effective strategy for making RL tractable for underactuated systems where a flat reward provides insufficient guidance through the state space.

## Conclusion

Reward engineering for underactuated systems under uncertainty is a structured design problem, not a tuning exercise. The Greedy-Divide and Conquer algorithm provides a principled decomposition that transforms a hard global objective into a sequence of tractable local problems. The method is applicable wherever the system dynamics create a natural sequential structure in the path from initial to target state — a common property of underactuated mechanical systems.
