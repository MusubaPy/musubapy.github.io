## Overview

A mobile robot sent into an unfamiliar room may have a target pose but no map of what stands between it and the goal. Repeated practice runs are also costly on physical hardware. This study combines local obstacle sensing with reinforcement learning that can update during a run, rather than waiting for a complete parking attempt.

## Methodology

The robot uses laser scans to detect obstacles and odometry to estimate its pose relative to the target. The controller fits lines and circles to groups of scan points and adds clearance buffers around them. These local constraints guide parking without a pre-built map. Map-less does not mean model-free: the controller still uses a motion model to predict the consequences of candidate commands.

**Stacked Q-learning (SQL)** uses the motion model to roll out a short sequence of candidate actions. Instead of adding up immediate stage costs along the predicted sequence, as in the tested model predictive control (MPC) baseline, SQL sums learned state–action cost estimates over the horizon. It applies the first action and replans from subsequent observations. The cost estimator, or critic, is updated periodically using recent transitions, so learning does not have to wait until an episode ends.

## Results

The comparison used accumulated parking cost, not path length alone. In [the simulations](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10845-023-02197-y/figures/7), SQL produced lower accumulated costs overall than the tested MPC controller. The [mean cost curves](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10845-023-02197-y/figures/8) show roughly **270 for SQL versus 330 for MPC** at about 90 seconds, but the advantage varied across the test area. The authors report the same computational complexity for the two methods; that is not a claim of identical wall-clock runtime.

The authors also tested on a [physical TurtleBot3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10845-023-02197-y/figures/6) and published [example trajectories for four targets](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10845-023-02197-y/figures/12). These examples demonstrate physical deployment; the reported simulation cost advantage should not be read as a general performance guarantee in new environments.

## Conclusion

Stacked Q-learning changes what a predictive controller optimises, while retaining the robot model and local obstacle constraints. The study shows that an online learned cost estimate can improve the tested simulation benchmark and can be used for parking on hardware without a prior map.
