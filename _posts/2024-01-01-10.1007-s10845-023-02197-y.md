## Overview

Deploying reinforcement learning on a physical mobile robot differs fundamentally from simulation: episodes cannot be run in large quantities, new environments may have no pre-built map, and the agent must learn online rather than after exhaustive offline training.
Standard model-free RL fails to address this regime — it requires many interactions and has no mechanism for exploiting partial model knowledge.
This paper presents **stacked Q-learning**, a predictive RL algorithm that bridges RL with model-based predictive agents to enable efficient online learning for map-less navigation.

## Approach

The algorithm operates without a global map, relying solely on laser scan data to detect obstacles in the robot's immediate vicinity. Rather than treating the navigation problem as a tabula-rasa episodic task, stacked Q-learning incorporates a short prediction horizon into the Q-function update, unifying the sample efficiency of model-based prediction with the flexibility of value-function learning.

The agent updates its policy continuously during operation — not only at episode boundaries — which is essential for physical deployment where episodes are expensive and failures are consequential. The model knowledge required is local and approximate: it does not demand a full system model, only a predictive structure within the value-function update.

## Results

The algorithm was evaluated on a mobile robot navigating to a target position while avoiding obstacles, using accumulated cost as the primary metric. The comparison baseline was a classical model predictive controller (MPC) with access to the same model information used by stacked Q-learning.

Stacked Q-learning outperformed MPC in terms of accumulated navigation cost while operating at equal computational complexity. The result confirms that incorporating a predictive horizon into the RL update yields measurable improvement over purely reactive MPC, without requiring additional computation.

## Conclusion

Stacked Q-learning demonstrates that the divide between RL and predictive control is not fundamental: a principled integration of a prediction horizon into the Q-function update produces an agent that is both online-capable and competitive with model-based controllers. The method provides a foundation for a class of predictive RL algorithms suited to physical robot deployment under time and resource constraints.
