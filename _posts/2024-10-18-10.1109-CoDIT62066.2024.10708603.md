## Overview

An underactuated robot has fewer independent inputs than degrees of freedom. A Pendubot, for example, has two links but only one actuated joint. Swinging both links upright requires moving through configurations that a reward based only on distance to the final pose may not encourage. Uncertain physical parameters complicate the design of a reward that guides those intermediate motions.

## Methodology

Greedy-Divide and Conquer divides the task into subtasks and uses them to design the reward. A [survey by several of the authors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10215v2) describes the method as partitioning the state space into regions and planning how the agent should move through them. Intermediate regions give the agent feedback before it reaches the final target. The Pendubot application uses one reinforcement learning agent for both swing-up and balance.

## Results

The [IEEE abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10708603) reports two examples: swinging up and balancing a Pendubot with uncertain parameters, and a planar drone with uncertain body mass. The drone example shows how the same reward-design idea can be applied to a different system and a specific uncertain parameter.

The abstract does not report task-level success rates or give a quantitative comparison against alternative rewards. The authors' survey discusses the [earlier Pendubot study](https://doi.org/10.1109/DCNA59899.2023.10290178) together with this paper, so the survey's Pendubot statistics cannot be attributed to the CoDIT results alone.

## Conclusion

The study offers a way to design rewards around intermediate actions that matter for an underactuated system. Its examples illustrate the method under parameter uncertainty without establishing a general robustness guarantee.
