## Overview

Quadruped robots can produce a range of gait patterns — walk, trot, canter, bound — each with different footfall sequences, duty cycles, and internal force distributions.
Selecting the appropriate gait for a given speed and task is a direct determinant of energy consumption, yet comparative evaluations across the full gait repertoire are sparse.
This paper addresses that gap by generating a representative set of gaits through trajectory optimisation and evaluating their energy cost under consistent conditions.

## Approach

Each gait is defined by its footfall pattern and timing parameters. Trajectories for all four legs are generated to satisfy kinematic constraints, ground-contact conditions, and stability criteria (static or dynamic, depending on the gait). The energy consumed per unit distance — quantified using the Specific Cost of Transport (CoT) — serves as the primary evaluation metric:

$$\text{CoT} = \frac{E}{mgd}$$

where $E$ is the mechanical energy expended, $m$ the robot mass, $g$ gravitational acceleration, and $d$ the distance travelled.

The study evaluates each gait across a range of walking speeds to characterise not only the absolute energy cost but also the speed-efficiency curve — the regime in which each gait is preferable to the alternatives.

## Results

The results show that slower, statically stable gaits (walking) are more efficient at low speeds, while dynamic gaits (trot, bound) become competitive or superior at higher speeds due to elastic energy storage mechanisms and more efficient momentum exchange. The crossover speed between gait optimality regions is identified for the tested quadruped configuration, providing a concrete selection criterion for gait scheduling algorithms.

The evaluation also quantifies the sensitivity of each gait's energy cost to changes in leg trajectory parameters, highlighting design choices that have the largest impact on efficiency.

## Conclusion

Matching gait selection to operating speed is a tractable energy optimisation problem once the gait-specific cost curves are known. This study provides those curves for a representative quadruped platform and establishes a principled framework for gait scheduling that minimises energy consumption across a mission profile. The results are directly applicable to the development of energy-aware locomotion planners for field robots with limited battery capacity.
